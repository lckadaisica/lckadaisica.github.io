4 STAGES:

Starbucks Brand Evolution

Starbucks Overview

STARBUCKS INTRODUCTION:

  • Created: March 30, 1971, Seattle
  • Originally Offered: Not a cafe Just specialty whole-bean coffee, tea, and spices (Small menu)
  • Currently Offering: A relaxed, sit-down cafe (free wifi, music, etc)

KNOWN FOR:

  • Making coffees
  • World’s largest coffee house chain
  • Espressos, Frappucino, Lattes, Pastries, Sandwiches, etc (Big menu)
  • Mobile Ordering App
  • Starbucks Reward Loyalty Program
  • Sweet drinks
  • Consistent overall experience
Target Audience: Starbucks’s original target audience was actually made for older people. However, over the years, they moved onto a younger part of the population.

VISUAL TIMELINE: 4 SIGNIFICANT STAGES

STAGE 1
Historical Context:
  • The company had lots of inspiration from their founding place, Seattle, the history of maritime and sea-faring
  • Targeted toward audience of middle age people
  • Enemies were independent local coffee shops → Nestle and Dunkin’ Brands
  • First store, 1 store at first, near Pike Place Market
  • Jerry Baldwin - English teacher, Gordon Bowker - Writer, Zev Siegl - History teacher→ original founders
  • Whole-bean coffee, loose-leaf tea, and spices = original products (not yet brewed beverages and lattes)
Logo:
  • Style: Wood carving of a two-tailed mermaid
  • Details: She was not wearing any clothes and looked detailed and very old-style
  • Shape: Inside of a circle frame
  • Designed: Terry Heckler
Typography:
  • Font Style: Thick, clean letters with no extra hooks (sans-serif)
  • Details: All capital letters, "STARBUCKS COFFEE TEA SPICES"
  • Spacing: Letters had wide spaces between them to look aesthetically appealing in a clean way
Colours:
  • Palette: Dark brown
  • Meaning: Connected to the colour of coffee beans
Intended Message:
  • Vibe: Vintage & Authentic
  • Goal: Told customers that this shop sold high-quality, imported ingredients for baking and brewing at home
STAGE 2: 2nd Logo
Historical context:
  • Howard Schultz bought Starbucks for 3.8 million dollars = merged it with his own coffee bar chain - Il Giornale
  • The company moved from only selling coffee beans, tea, and spices to espressos, lattes, and brewed coffee
  • Only 11 to 17 stores at the time → led to rapid regional and national expansion - opening locations in Chicago and Vancouver
  • The audience moved from culinary coffee lovers to everyday people looking for a relaxing café
Logo:
  • Decided the bare-chested mermaid was too risky for public view, so they switched to her hair covering her bare chest
  • She was on a black circle, framed by a thick outer green ring containing text that says Starbucks Coffee (it removed the words “Tea” and “Spices”), 2 stars separated the words on the left and right sides
  • The frame & shape remained a circle
  • The palette switched from brown to green
  • Designed by Doug Fast and Terry Heckler
Typography:
  • used a bold sans-serif typeface
  • Lettering remained all caps and set inside the outer ring
  • The letterings resembled Franklin Gothic font
Palette:
  • Introduced the colour of emerald green into the outer circle, black in the backdrop, and the mermaid is white, to keep high contrast between the mermaid and the background
  • From the original 1971 earthy "coffee brown" change to the green palette was from the Il Giornale company colours → Green = growth, freshness, prosperity, new beginning
Intended Message:
  • The design signaled a change from local, gritty seaport shop to clean, modern brand
  • The updated siren looked more professional and welcoming
  • The new look appealed directly to 1980s urban workers looking for a daily café
STAGE 3
Historical Context:
  • Company went public in 1992, on NASDAQ stock exchange to fund new expansions
  • Store count = goes beyond 160 locations all across North America big cities
  • Grew outside traditional cafes, placing legal small corners in grocery stores, bookstores, etc
Logo:
  • Design zoomed in, cutting points below her chest-ish belly, cropping her split fishtails so that only some of it stayed visible on the sides
  • Became cleaner and much less cluttered by shifting focus to her smiling face and wavy hair
Typography:
  • The lettering stayed with the 1987 bold, geometric, all-caps sans-serif
  • The text grew slightly within the outer green border due to the cropped parts in the center
  • The font style stayed the same
Palette:
  • The palette stayed the exact same from Stage 2
  • Contrast got better because of zoomed in details that reduce some of the intricate illustrations of the mermaid, white on black still but better contrast overall
  • Colour = now globally recognized as “Starbucks Green”
Intended Message:
  • Hidden her body to make the emblem look even more family-friendly
  • The closer framing emphasized a personal, welcoming by highlighting the mermaid’s face
  • The refined look showed corporate modernization by making it look cleaner overall
STAGE 4 (LAST STAGE WOOHOO)
Historical context:
  • The company expanded globally into non-coffee consumer products, selling drinks, ice cream, and other things in grocery stores
  • The digital era arrived → digital rewards programs, and small mobile screens
  • The brand evolved beyond traditional café walls
Logo:
  • Outer ring = removed, removing the circular border, the stars, and the words "STARBUCKS COFFEE."
  • The mermaid was switched onto against solid green background
  • Asymmetry was added to her nose and eyes designed by Lippincott to make her face look more human and less perfect
Typography:
  • They removed the letters
Palette:
  • palette = just 2 colours: Starbucks green and white
  • The solid black background behind the siren was completely erased
  • The iconic emerald green took center stage for the brand's identity
Intended Message:
  • Wordless design = no longer just a coffee company → they sell teas and other food freely now
  • Borderless = a modern view that doesn't need its name on there to be recognizable
  • Icon = make online appearance better, making the logo even cleaner, and recognizable on tiny mobile app icons
